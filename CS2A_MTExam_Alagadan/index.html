Tourist Spots in Ilocos Sur

Calle Crisologo, Vigan City

Calle Crisologo in Vigan is a timeless street filled with Spanish-era houses and cobblestone roads. Walking here feels like stepping back into the colonial past of the Philippines. It is a must-visit destination for history lovers and photographers.

Santa Maria Church

Sta. Maria Church, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, stands proudly on top of a hill. Built in the 18th century, its baroque architecture and massive buttresses highlight its beauty. It is both a sacred place and a symbol of Ilocos Sur’s rich history.

Vitalis Villas, Santiago

Vitalis Villas in Santiago, Ilocos Sur is known as the “Santorini of the North.” Its whitewashed villas, overlooking the blue sea, offer the perfect escape for relaxation. Guests come here for the stunning views and luxury experience.

Famous Local Products

Vigan Longganisa

₱150 / 12pcs

Vigan Longganisa is a small garlicky sausage famous for its strong and savory flavor. Best paired with rice and vinegar, it is a breakfast favorite among locals. Tourists often bring it home as pasalubong.

Bagnet

₱300 / kilo

Bagnet is a deep-fried pork belly that is irresistibly crispy outside yet tender inside. It is often served with vegetables like pinakbet or enjoyed with rice alone. This dish is one of the most famous specialties in Ilocos.

Balikutsa

₱170

A Balikutsa is a traditional Ilocano candy made from sugarcane. The melted sugar is pulled and twisted into spirals or sticks. It is a sweet reminder of the region’s simple yet delicious treats.

Banig

₱500

Banig is a colorful handwoven mat made of palm or reed leaves. It is used for sleeping or sitting and is valued for its beautiful designs. Owning one is like bringing home a piece of Ilocano tradition.

Abel

₱250

Abel is a handwoven textile proudly made in Ilocos. Known for its durability and colorful patterns, it represents the artistry of local weavers. Many tourists buy it as souvenirs or gifts.

Bibingka

₱30

Native Bibingka is a soft rice cake cooked over charcoal. Made with coconut milk and sugar, it has a distinct smoky and sweet taste. It is usually enjoyed during snacks or festive occasions.

Burnay

₱1000

Burnay jars are earthenware pots handmade in Vigan. They are traditionally used for storing food, water, and vinegar. Today, they are also collected as unique home decorations.

Calamay

₱100/3packs

Calamay is a sticky rice delicacy sweetened with coconut milk and sugar. It is often packed in coconut shells or banana leaves. This treat is both filling and flavorful, perfect as pasalubong.

Chicharon

₱75

Chicharon is a crunchy snack made of fried pork skin. It is often dipped in vinegar for extra flavor. In Ilocos, it is a popular pasalubong item for visitors.

Ilocos Empanada

₱100 / kilo

Ilocos Empanada is a crunchy orange pastry filled with egg, vegetables, and longganisa. It is fried until crisp and served with Sukang Iloko. Many say your Ilocos trip is not complete without tasting this street food.

Sukang Iloko

₱120

Sukang Iloko is a sugarcane vinegar with a bold, tangy taste. It is commonly used in Ilocano dishes and as a dipping sauce. Its strong flavor makes every meal more exciting.

Pinakbet

₱100 / pack

Pinakbet is a famous Ilocano vegetable dish cooked with bagoong. It combines bitter melon, eggplant, and other vegetables into one savory meal. This dish reflects the healthy and flavorful cuisine of the region.
